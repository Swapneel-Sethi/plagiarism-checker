Multiple execution units operating simultaneously may attempt to modify shared state, producing inconsistent outcomes if left unmanaged. Synchronization primitives provide the necessary guarantees to ensure correctness under contention. The selection of an appropriate primitive influences both safety and performance. Implementations must balance the overhead of coordination against the risk of race conditions that compromise data integrity.

Consensus algorithms allow a set of nodes to agree on a shared value despite the presence of faulty participants. The theoretical foundations of these algorithms establish bounds on the conditions under which agreement is attainable.
